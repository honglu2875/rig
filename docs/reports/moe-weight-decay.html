02 · paired evidence

0.3 is not a lucky-seed winner at 125M.

The paired differences stay above zero for every alternative coefficient and every seed. The local basin is shallow, but its location is robust across this grid.

The recipe applies both a global horizon factor and tensor-role multipliers:

lr = base_lr · √(mB/mD) · tensor_lr
wd = base_wd · √(mB/mD) · tensor_wd

At 125M, √(mB/mD) = 0.696662. For hidden matrices at base WD 0.3, peak LR = 0.00163280 and WD = 0.348331, giving 0.00056876 peak shrink per step. There are 2.06× as many steps as at 60M, which almost exactly offsets it.

CompleteP contribution

Hidden, router, and expert matrices use width-scaled LR and inverse-compensating decay; embeddings and output projection follow their declared tensor roles.

Complete(d)P contribution

The project borrows the global √(mB/mD) multiplier. Here mD is the dense-equivalent active ladder ratio, not stored MoE capacity.

Scope of inference

Neither dense paper establishes this routed extension. The sweep validates an operational choice at these tiers; it does not prove a universal MoE decay law.

Working decision

Use base weight decay 0.3 as the 125M-specific working choice; keep 0.1 as the cross-tier default for now. The 125M gain is paired and robust. The 60M result is too entangled with one spike to justify either declaring non-transfer or globally changing the recipe. If this question matters again, spend runs on more 60M seeds at 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3—not on another wider 125M bracket.
