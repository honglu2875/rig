The dictionary wakes, then early layers go quiet.

Fuzzy TopK's 16D dictionary is fully explored at initialization, but roughly one third of stored features become persistently inactive. The effect concentrates just after the first block and strengthens with model scale—even though this same architecture improves language-model loss.

The main shape

Inactivity is created during training and is organized by depth

Almost every feature is positive at least once in the first 131,072-token sampled batch; the handful of exceptions appear by step 10. By the endpoint, however, 34–36% of features per layer are absent from ten consecutive captures on average. Block 0 remains broadly active. Block 1 is the sharp bottleneck, after which coverage recovers through depth.

Data and evidence boundary

Full cadence is preserved; browser detail widens only in time

Authoritative source

fuzzy_sparsity.rigvec stores four float32 vectors for every layer and feature at step 1, every ten steps, and final: 40.19 GiB across 12 runs. The ordinary training log is byte-identical to the prior uninstrumented fuzzy run for every 60M–250M seed.

Interactive companion

The 0.891 GiB companion keeps every feature value through step 200, then at 300, 500, 900, 1700, and doubled gaps plus final. The study browser adds dead-layer heatmaps, time ridgelines, draggable histograms, and positive-frequency quantiles.

Three dead definitions

Current-batch zero measures instantaneous support. Trailing-ten zero detects later persistent inactivity. Ever-observed zero answers only whether a feature has activated at least once since initialization and therefore cannot diagnose later death.

What this does not establish

Observer batches sample the corpus; “dead” is not global. Feature identities are fixed within a run but not aligned across seeds. The study measures dictionary use and does not test whether reviving, pruning, or reallocating inactive features improves loss.
